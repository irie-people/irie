# Irie for Mac

Date: 2026-10-26. Status: first build. Read with [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

Irie for Mac is a native app for the people who would never open a terminal, and it stays useful for developers. It is a SwiftUI front end to the same core as the terminal sidebar. The core still runs every agent in tmux, so agents keep working when the app quits. Linux, and anyone who prefers the terminal, keeps the terminal sidebar.

## 1. What it shows

- **A card per session, in a tree.** The **State | Repo** switch above the details panel picks the view, and the app keeps the choice. It opens in **Repo**, a tree:
  - each repo, by the name of its folder (with the folder above it when two repos share a name), in the order of its first session in the list;
  - under it, its sessions in the list's order, each followed by its helpers. The list's order is the core's one order, which the terminal sidebar draws too (DESIGN §4.1): the order the sessions started, until you drag a card or promote one;
  - then **Not in a repo**, then **Outside Irie**.

  A state change never moves a card here (DESIGN §4.1); it only changes the card's mark and colours. **State** groups the sessions into **Needs you**, **Working** and **Done**, in the list's order within each group, so a card moves to another group when its state changes. A group's header shows its count; a click folds it, and a folded group says in red how many of its sessions need you. The app keeps what is folded. While the find field has text, every group is open, so what it finds shows. Each card is rounded and soft, washed in its agent's own colour while it works and tinted and edged in red while it needs you. The cards lie flat in the list, with no edge or shadow at rest, like a window's other tabs, so the list stays calm; the selected card stands up from them on a gentle shadow, and the one under the pointer takes a faint accent edge. The rows outside Irie lie flat the same way.
- **A short top.** The compact title bar, 38 points tall, holds the traffic lights and, over the sidebar, Irie's sun and name, then the counts of how many need you, are working or are done; each count jumps to the first such session. Under it is one row, the find field at full width, and then the first card: 70 points from the top of the window, where the first build took 133. The title bar has no sidebar button, so all of it fits beside a sidebar at its narrowest, 260 points; with more counts than fit, the counts go into the title bar's overflow menu and the name stays. View > Hide Sidebar (⌃⌘S) hides the sidebar. The shown session's name starts 24 points from the sidebar's edge. It and the line under it are the toolbar's principal item, drawn once: the bar hides the window's own title.
- **Cards that never move.** Every card is two lines: the name, then what the session needs from you (in red, on a red tint), what it is doing, or how it ended, in plain words: "Running the tests", "Editing main.rs", "Asks: may it run rm -rf build?". A finished jot whose work has not landed says what is left in the accent's text colour, "Not through: 2 commits not in live", and one that waits on its own work in the background says so, where the others say "Done" (DESIGN §4.8). The exact call, such as `Bash · go test ./...`, is in the details panel. Its age and token count sit in a trailing slot of fixed width. Hovering swaps that slot for quick actions (pause or resume, promote, and ⋯ for the rest; a click on the card types to it), so the name never re-wraps and no card changes height under the pointer.
- **A details panel**, a card above the task bar, for the selected session: its state, current step, profile and branch, folder, tokens, helpers, warnings, and the last screen of a spawn that went wrong. Long lines are cut off, and the full text shows on hover. It opens small, the state and one line, and the cards have the rest; at any height it shows as many whole lines as fit. Drag its top edge to make it taller, as far as leaves the cards a group's header and two cards, or shorter; the pointer turns to the up-down arrow there. Its chevron, a double click on that edge or a drag to the bottom minimizes it to one line: what the session needs from you, or else the call its agent is making, or what it is doing. The app keeps its height and whether it is minimized. VoiceOver adjusts the height as **Details height**.
- **Helpers.** A session's running subagents sit under its card, a line each: its kind and what it is doing, in plain words and in red when it needs you. Three show, then how many more. The panel lists them too. The core reports them in each row's `subagents`, kept from the agent's SubagentStart and SubagentStop hooks.
- **Tokens per session.** Each card shows the session's token count, and the panel splits it into input, output, cache read and cache write. The core reads each row's `tokens` from the session's transcript and its finished subagents' transcripts. It counts each reply once, because a reply spans several lines that all repeat its usage.
- **Accessibility.** VoiceOver reads each card as one element: name, state, what it needs or does, when it started ("started 3 minutes ago") and its tokens. The card's actions offer what the hover buttons and the right-click menu do. Notices are announced, and errors stay until the next notice or until you edit or submit. Text colours keep 4.5:1 contrast, with stronger ones for Increase Contrast. The selected card always shows the selection's full fill; its edge runs bolder while the list has the keyboard.
- **The shown session's conversation** on the right, by default (DESIGN §4.7). You and Claude take turns: your messages on soft cards, Claude's replies in Markdown. Each tool call is one line in plain words ("Ran the tests ✓ 40 s"), with how long it took and the lines an edit changed, and opens to its exact command or path, or a failed call to its output. Two or more reads, edits or searches in a row gather into one line ("Edited 3 files +42 −7"). Subagents are chips with their kind and task, and what they do now while they run. A session that started before Irie followed conversations says so and offers its terminal. The view follows the selection and keeps to the newest turn while you are at the bottom.
- **A message box** at the foot of the conversation types to the agent through the core, the way a person would (`send`): Return sends it, and an agent at work reads it at its next step. It shows as pending until the agent takes it. While the agent waits on you in its terminal (a permission, a question, a plan), a banner offers the terminal instead of the box, and a card at the end says what it asks, with a question's choices or the plan. Answering still happens in the terminal.
- **The shown agent's live terminal** behind the conversation, a flip away (**View > Terminal**, ⌥⌘T, or **Terminal** under the message box). It stays attached while the conversation shows, so the flip is instant; the choice is remembered. The terminal sits in a rounded frame edged in the colour round that session's card: red while it needs you, green once it is done, its own colour while it works, and sun otherwise. Two lines in the same colour, a little apart, run straight across from the middle of the card's trailing edge to the frame. Each turns into both edges on a curve, the upper line up and the lower one down, like a U over an upside-down U, and both edges open between them, so the card and its terminal read as one outline. They show while that stretch of the card is in sight, and narrow from the card's bolder edge while the list has the keyboard. Selecting a row swaps that agent in, and Return (or **Type to it**) moves the keyboard to the message box, or to the terminal while it shows.
- **A task bar** at the foot of the sidebar, always three lines tall; longer text scrolls inside it. It shares one panel with the **State | Repo** switch and the details panel above it, lighter than the list, with rounded top corners. Beside the text, a menu picks where a task runs: a repo the `@` words know (a new worktree of it), a folder chosen with **Choose a Folder…**, or **Not in a repo** (a new scratch folder). The app keeps the choice. The bar takes the same `@repo`, `@repo!` and `+profile` words as the terminal sidebar (DESIGN §4.2), and an `@repo` in the text wins over the menu, which then shows it. It keeps the keyboard while a task starts, and after: a new agent starts behind the session on show, so a task typed mid-work never pulls the view away, and its card joins the list. Only when no session is on show does the app show the new agent and give its terminal the keyboard. Unsent text stays in the bar when the app is stopped and relaunched, as every install does. Notices float above it rather than pushing the list.
- **Four palettes**, in View > Theme (`Theme.swift`):
  - **Rosewater**, the default: blush and cream, rose accents, plum ink.
  - **Terracotta**: oat and peach, terracotta accents, cocoa-plum ink.
  - **Lilac**: lavender cream, orchid accents, plum ink.
  - **Evergreen**: ivory and sage, deep forest-green accents, brass sun, moss-black ink.

  Each has a light and a dark set, sage for done and the sun and waves of Irie's mark. The grounds are solid, so nothing behind the window shows through. In every palette and mode, text keeps 4.5:1 and marks 3:1 on every surface they sit on, and Increase Contrast moves text further from the grounds and hairlines toward the ink (WCAG 2.2). Cards and fields are rounded, in rounded type; the fields and the selected card sit on gentle shadows, and the other cards lie flat. The app keeps the choice; changing it draws the sidebar afresh while the terminal stays attached.
- **Pause and Resume.** A card's pause button (on hover, ⏸) or **Pause** in its menu freezes the session's whole process tree at once: the agent, its helpers and everything they started (DESIGN §4.3). The card shows ◆ and **Frozen** at once, and its terminal takes no keys. **Resume** (▶ in the same place) carries everything on, and an agent that was at work hears how long it was paused at its next step. A pause holds through restarts of the app and the core, and installs. An agent that started before this build ran under an older host, which would undo a stop of the agent itself: everything it started stops at once, the agent stops at its next tool call, and until then its card says **Freezing…**.
- **Promote.** A card's promote button (↑ in a circle) or **Promote to the Top** in its menu puts the session first in the list, with the same mark as a badge after its name, and gives its whole process tree the machine first, before the call returns: every other session runs in macOS's background state, with the lowest CPU priority and throttled disk and network I/O, and so does what they start. Spawns and typed messages it asks for go first. **Stop Promoting** takes the others out of the background and leaves the card where it is. macOS gives no way to set memory aside for one process, so the details panel says so plainly; the background processes are the ones macOS slows first.
- **Drag to reorder.** Drag a card up or down within its group, and it goes where it is dropped; a line shows where. **Move Up** and **Move Down** in its menu, and among VoiceOver's actions, do the same without a drag. The order is the core's (`reorder`), so the terminal sidebar shows it too, and it survives restarts. A state change still never moves a card in the Repo view.
- **Right-click on a row,** or its ⋯ button: show and type to it, open it in Ghostty, rename it, pause or resume it, promote it, move it up or down, show the folder in Finder, copy the folder path, branch name or session ID, and close it.
- **Renaming a session.** Double-click its card, or choose **Rename…** from the card's ⋯ or right-click menu or **File > Rename Session…**, and the name opens to edit in place, at its own size, so the card keeps its height. Return renames the session, Esc keeps the name, and clicking elsewhere renames it, as in Finder. A name can be any text: the core makes runs of spaces one space and numbers a name another session has (`rename`, DESIGN §2.4). The name is the core's, so it stays through restarts of the app and the core, and through installs. VoiceOver offers **Rename** among the card's actions.
- **Sessions close like tabs.** Close Session (⌘W, or Close in a row's menu) closes it at once, without asking, and the next session in the list takes its place. The core swaps its pane out, ends its agent gently and removes its row; its worktree and folder stay (DESIGN §4.5). Reopen Closed Session (⇧⌘T) is the undo: the session comes back in its place in the list, carrying its conversation on.
- **Outside Irie.** Claude Code sessions that run in other terminals or in the background follow the cards, under a header with their count (DESIGN §4.6). Each is one line, in the order it started, with the mark of the state it matches, so a state change never moves or resizes it. A click or the arrow keys select one, and the details panel shows what it is doing, where it runs and its folder, with **Move into Irie** and **Copy Session ID**. While the session is busy or waits for you, the button is off and the panel says why. A move asks first, because the session's copy outside Irie ends, and anything it runs in the background stops. Then the conversation carries on in an Irie pane, and its terminal takes the keyboard. Right-click a row for the same, and to show or copy its folder. The keys that show a session skip these rows, since Irie can't show them.

## 2. How the terminal gets into the window

The app adds no state of its own to the core. It uses two additions to the socket API (DESIGN §2.4):

- `subscribe` with `view: "app"`. While at least one such subscription is open, the core keeps the slot zoomed (`resize-pane -Z`), so the slot fills `irie:0`. Swaps keep the zoom (`swap-pane -Z`, `select-pane -Z`). Agent windows take the zoomed slot's size, so a swap still never resizes an agent. When the last app view closes, the zoom ends and the sidebar is back.
- `attach-info`, which returns the tmux binary, the server's socket path and the session to attach to.

The app runs `tmux -S <socket> attach-session -t irie` inside its terminal view. Because the slot is zoomed, that client shows only the shown agent, and `show` swaps agents in place.

Two details of the generated tmux.conf follow from this. The `window-resized` hook leaves a zoomed window alone, because resizing the sidebar would end the zoom. In a terminal attached to the zoomed slot, the focus key goes to the agent, because there is no sidebar pane to move to. The app catches Ctrl+] itself (§4), so from the app it never reaches tmux. While the app is open, a terminal attached with `irie attach` also sees only the slot.

## 3. Build and run

`mac/build.sh` builds `mac/build/Irie.app`. It calls `swiftc` directly because SwiftPM 5.9, the version in the Command Line Tools for macOS 14.1, stops at `unable to lookup item 'PlatformPath'`. `tools/live.sh` (AGENTS.md) runs it when `mac/` or `tools/mkicon` changed, installs the app in `~/Applications`, registers it again so Finder and the Dock show its current icon, and relaunches it if it was running.

The icon is Irie's mark, a warm sun rising over two calm waves on a deep-green rounded square (DESIGN §7.2). `go run ./tools/mkicon` draws it with Go's standard library and writes the ten PNGs of an iconset, and the script packs them into `Irie.icns` with `iconutil`, so the build needs Go as well. macOS draws an icns as it is, so the icon carries its own shape: an 824-pixel square with continuous corners in the middle of the 1024-pixel canvas, on a soft shadow. Below 64 pixels the rays would blur into the sun, so the small sizes draw a larger sun over two level lines, with their edges on whole pixels. The output is the same on every run, which its tests check.

The terminal view is [SwiftTerm](https://github.com/migueldeicaza/SwiftTerm) v1.18.0 (MIT), the newest release whose manifest builds with Swift 5.9. The script fetches it, refuses the download unless its SHA-256 matches, and compiles it once into a static library in `~/Library/Caches/irie` (or `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/irie`), named for the release, the target and the compiler. Every checkout and worktree shares it, so a fresh worktree's build compiles only Irie's own files. SwiftTerm draws with Metal: its renderer compiles `Shaders.metal`, which the script copies into the app, when the app runs, so no Xcode is needed. Where Metal is missing it draws with Core Graphics.

`Irie --snapshot DIR` draws the window offscreen from fixture sessions in `/home/alice`, never a core. It writes a PNG of each palette in light and dark mode at the default size, such as `rosewater-light.png`; `window-narrow.png` and `window-wide.png` at 820 and 1500 points; and `details-minimized.png` and `details-tall.png`, with the details panel minimized and dragged as tall as it goes. It writes them to DIR, prints the title bar's height and where the first card starts, in points, and exits. It shows the terminal view, whichever view the app has saved, with an empty terminal in the frame, so the frame and the lines from the selected card show. It is how a change to the views is checked without a screen. The window is ordered in far off any screen, so it never shows, and the app takes no focus. The picture is drawn with `cacheDisplay`, which gives a fully rounded edge a short straight piece at each end that the screen does not show.

`Irie --render-conversation DIR` draws the conversation view offscreen with SwiftUI's ImageRenderer, from fixture items in `/home/alice`, never a core: `conversation-<palette>-<light|dark>.png` for each palette, and `waiting-…png` for a session that waits on you. ImageRenderer leaves out AppKit's own controls, such as the text field, so the rendering draws the field's placeholder in its place; `--snapshot` shows the same fixtures in the real window.

`IRIE_MAC_NO_SWIFTTERM=1 mac/build.sh` builds with Apple's frameworks only. The terminal then opens in a Ghostty window that follows the selected session. Where SwiftPM works (Xcode, or newer Command Line Tools), `mac/Package.swift` builds the same app.

The app finds the core the way `irie` does (`internal/paths`). `--socket PATH`, or `IRIE_CORE_SOCKET`, points it at another Irie. It needs a core with `attach-info`; an older one gets a message to run the new build's `irie up`.

## 4. Keys

The **File**, **Go**, **View** and **Edit** menus list them all.

| Keys | What they do |
|---|---|
| Ctrl+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab | Show the next or the previous session in the sidebar's order: group by group, without the cards in folded groups or those the filter hides, wrapping at the ends. From a card in a folded group it goes on from that card's place. Presses faster than the core answers collapse into the last one, so the terminal always ends on the highlighted session. The keyboard stays where it was. The app catches these keys before the terminal or AppKit's key-view loop sees them |
| Ctrl+] | Move the keyboard between the session list and the message box, or the terminal while it shows |
| ⌥⌘T | Show the live terminal instead of the conversation, and back (View > Terminal) |
| Return, ⌥Return | In the message box: send it, or start a new line |
| ⌘1 to ⌘9 | Show the first to ninth session in the sidebar's order |
| ⌘F | Find a session. Esc clears the search and goes back to the list |
| ⌃⌘S | Hide or show the sidebar |
| ⌘N | New task: a sheet with the repo, whether the task gets its own worktree, the agent (every `+` profile, auto routing and the local models, each with what it runs and costs), the model, and the text. A model applies to a Claude Code profile: its own, or the latest Fable, Opus or Sonnet. The `@repo` and `+agent` words in the text win over the menus. ⌘Return starts it, and the repo chosen becomes the task bar's |
| ⌥⌘N | Type a task in the task bar |
| ⌘W | Close the selected session, even while the terminal has the keyboard. The app catches it first |
| ⇧⌘T | Reopen the session closed last |
| ⇧⌘W | Close the window. Agents keep running, and the app quits with it |
| ↑ and ↓, then Return | Pick a session in the list, then type to it. The arrows go on into Outside Irie, where they only select a row, and Return says that it runs outside Irie |
| ⌘C, ⌘V, ⌘A | Copy, paste and select all in the terminal. A mouse drag selects in tmux, which puts the text on the pasteboard when you let go (tmux's `set-clipboard external` and OSC 52, which SwiftTerm writes to the pasteboard). ⌘C copies a selection made with Shift-drag, which bypasses tmux |
| ⌘=, ⌘-, ⌘0 | Make the terminal's text bigger, smaller or the default size |

## 5. States and colours

Each card's mark is its state's glyph from DESIGN §5.3, drawn: `✕` failed, `·` exited (a dim red `✕` when the exit code is not 0), `◆` frozen, `‖` paused, `●` needs you, `!` stalled, a dim `‖` pausing, `◐` working, `◌` starting, `✓` done and `○` idle. Their colours are §5.3's roles in the palette in use: red is the palette's needs colour, cyan its frozen, yellow its paused, green its done, the default its ink and dim its muted text. Only what needs you is red, and no state is told apart by colour alone. The title bar counts sessions that need you (needs you, stalled and failed), that are working, that are done and not yet seen, and paused and frozen ones when there are any, each with its glyph.

While a session works it has a colour of its own, so a glance tells the agents apart: its mark, and a wash across its card, fullest by the mark and fading toward the trailing slot. The six colours fan over the half of the colour wheel opposite the palette's accent, leaving out 30° either side of the needs red, and each session keeps one by its ID. A wash goes only as far as keeps the card's text at 4.5:1 and leaves its mark room for 3:1, and each mark's brightness is found for 3:1 on its wash and on the selection. A selected card is a gradient instead, so it stands out and keeps its colour: from the selection's fill by the mark into its wash, then deeper across the trailing slot, toward its mark's colour that edges its terminal, as far as keeps the slot's age, tokens and buttons, drawn in ink there, at 4.5:1. Its edge takes its mark's colour, as do the lines from it and the terminal's frame, and runs bolder while the list has the keyboard. Its text keeps 4.5:1 all along the gradient, and its mark, and so its edge, 3:1 as far as the trailing slot. Under Increase Contrast every card keeps its plain fill. The colour follows the session away from its card too. Its helpers under the card, the details panel (its mark, its state, its lines and its edge) and the subtitle above its terminal are drawn in it, the text bright or deep enough for 4.5:1 on every ground it sits on (7:1 under Increase Contrast). A warning, a helper that needs you, and how pause and promote stand keep their own colours. The app tells the core its palette (the `theme` op, on connecting and on every change), so Irie's status line in the terminal draws the working state and the rest of its line in the same colour (DESIGN §6.3).

Before the palettes, the app did not follow §5.3:

- The title bar's counts were three identical dots, told apart by colour alone.
- Working was blue; §5.3 gives it the default colour.
- Failed and stalled were drawn as needs you, a red dot, so three states shared one glyph. The details panel called all three "Needs you" and an exit "Ended".
- An exit with an error code looked like a clean one: a grey dot instead of red.
- Starting was a blue dashed ring instead of a dim one.
- The done count also counted idle and exited sessions, which the terminal sidebar does not.
- Frozen, paused and pausing had no look at all. The core derives them from milestones 5 and 6, and they are drawn now.

With `ui.red_on_done` set, the core's header says so and the app draws done in red, as the terminal sidebar does.

## 6. Not yet

These come with the core features they need, or after:

- park controls, notes to an agent, and undo beyond reopening a closed session (M4 to M7);
- buttons that answer a question or approve a command;
- choosing a local model for small jobs;
- a signed, notarized app.
