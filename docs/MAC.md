# Irie for Mac

Date: 2026-10-26. Status: first build. Read with [DESIGN.md](DESIGN.md).

Irie for Mac is a native app for the people who would never open a terminal, and it stays useful for developers. It is a SwiftUI front end to the same core as the terminal sidebar. The core still runs every agent in tmux, so agents keep working when the app quits. Linux, and anyone who prefers the terminal, keeps the terminal sidebar.

## 1. What it shows

- **A card per session, in a list or a tree.** The **List | Repo** switch above the details panel picks the view, and the app keeps the choice. **List** is one list of cards in the list's order, with no header. **Repo**, where it opens, is a tree:
  - each repo, by the name of its folder (with the folder above it when two repos share a name), in the order of its first session in the list;
  - under it, its sessions in the list's order, each followed by its helpers;
  - then **Not in a repo**.

  Both end with **Parked**, then a group for each paired machine's sessions, such as **On Asahi** (DESIGN §2.8), in the order they have there, then **Outside Irie**. A machine's cards leave out the "on Asahi" their details start with, since the header says it; parked, one goes into Parked with the rest and keeps it. The list's order is the core's one order, which the terminal sidebar draws too (DESIGN §4.1): the order the sessions started, until you drag a card or park one. Neither a state change nor a boost ever moves a card; it only changes the card's mark and colours. The State view of earlier builds, which moved a card to another group whenever its state changed, is gone, and a saved choice of it opens **List**. A group's header shows its count; a click folds it, and a folded group says in red how many of its sessions need you. **Parked** starts folded, so a card parked goes out of sight. The app keeps what is folded, and Parked open once you open it. While the find field has text, every group is open, so what it finds shows. Each card is rounded and soft, washed in its agent's own colour while it works and tinted and edged in red while it needs you. The cards lie flat in the list, with no edge or shadow at rest, like a window's other tabs, so the list stays calm; the selected card stands up from them on a gentle shadow, and the one under the pointer takes a faint accent edge. The rows outside Irie lie flat the same way.
- **A short top.** The compact title bar, 38 points tall, holds the traffic lights and, over the sidebar, Irie's sun and name, then the counts of how many need you, are working or wait for you to verify, where a parked session is not counted; each count jumps to the first such session. Under it is one row, the find field at full width, and then the first card: 70 points from the top of the window, where the first build took 133. The title bar has no sidebar button, so all of it fits beside a sidebar at its narrowest, 260 points; with more counts than fit, the counts go into the title bar's overflow menu and the name stays. View > Hide Sidebar (⌃⌘S) hides the sidebar. The shown session's name starts 24 points from the sidebar's edge. It and the line under it are the toolbar's principal item, drawn once: the bar hides the window's own title.
- **Cards that never move.** Every card is two lines: the name, then what the session needs from you (in red, on a red tint), what it is doing, or how it ended, in plain words: "Running the tests", "Editing main.rs", "Asks: may it run rm -rf build?". A finished jot whose work has not landed says what is left in the accent's text colour, "Not through: 2 commits not in live", and one that waits on its own work in the background says so, where the others say "To verify" (DESIGN §4.8). The exact call, such as `Bash · go test ./...`, is in the details panel. Once a session's prompt-cache rebuilds have cost `usage.rebuild_card` in a day, a third line says so with the price first, "$4.10 today: woke cold 3 times" (DESIGN §6.3), and wraps onto a fourth on a narrow card rather than cut the price; the details panel has today's and the week's. Its age and token count sit in a trailing slot of fixed width. Hovering swaps that slot for quick actions (pause or resume, boost, park or unpark, and ⋯ for the rest; a click on the card types to it), so the name never re-wraps and no card changes height under the pointer. Once a frozen session's cache has run out, Resume's help and menu item say what waking it costs, "~$2.40 to wake", and so does the review dock's Reopen for a closed one. A boosted card keeps its boost button there, filled, beside its age, as the one mark that it is boosted.
- **A details panel**, a card above the task bar, for the selected session: its state, current step, profile and branch, folder, tokens, helpers, warnings, and the last screen of a spawn that went wrong. Long lines are cut off, and the full text shows on hover. It opens small, the state and one line, and the cards have the rest; at any height it shows as many whole lines as fit. Drag its top edge to make it taller, as far as leaves the cards a group's header and two cards, or shorter; the pointer turns to the up-down arrow there. Its chevron, a double click on that edge or a drag to the bottom minimizes it to one line: what the session needs from you, or else the call its agent is making, or what it is doing. The app keeps its height and whether it is minimized. VoiceOver adjusts the height as **Details height**.
- **Helpers.** A session's running subagents sit under its card, a line each: its kind and what it is doing, in plain words and in red when it needs you. Three show, then how many more. The panel lists them too. The core reports them in each row's `subagents`, kept from the agent's SubagentStart and SubagentStop hooks.
- **The right sidebar.** A column at the right of the window with tabs that are the same whichever session shows (docs/proposals/right-dock.md): what is a session's own, its controls and its helpers, is a card in the panes beside its terminal instead (below). **View > Show Right Sidebar** (⌥⌘B, the key cmux and VS Code give a right side bar) or the toolbar's last button shows and hides it, and each tab has a key of its own, listed in View under **Right Sidebar** with a tick by the tab on show: ⌥⌘3 **Helpers** (yours), ⌥⌘5 **Review** and ⌥⌘6 **Notices** so far, since the numbers keep room for tabs to come; ⌥⌘1 stays free since the Dock became a session's card. **Variorum**, the Writing plugin's tab (⌥⌘8, docs/proposals/writers.md), shows only when Variorum is installed or set up. It opens on Helpers the first time. Nothing opens it or switches its tab but you: what your helpers work on only puts a count on its tab, red while one needs you. It keeps its tab and width as you go from session to session, and the app keeps whether it is open, its width and its tab. It is a drawer at the window's right edge, and sits after the terminal or the conversation: opening it slides it in over the terminal's edge, and once it is in, the terminal narrows to make room, in one step; closing it widens the terminal first, under the drawer, and then slides the drawer out. Only the drawer moves, so the terminal's width changes once each time, never at each step of the slide, and every agent follows once, as when the window is resized, since every agent's window takes the terminal's size (DESIGN §2.3). With Reduce Motion on, it opens and closes in one step. In a window that would leave the terminal less than about 80 columns, the sidebar gives up width first, down to its narrowest. Drag its leading edge to change its width, from 260 to 520 points: a line shows where it will go, and the width applies when you let go, so the agents are resized once, not on every move; a double click puts back its first width, 340 points. Esc in the sidebar hands the keyboard back to the terminal or the message box, and × in its header closes it.
- **The toolbar's three buttons**, at the right of the title bar: **Show Helpers** (⇧⌘H), with the Helpers card's icon, turns the panes beside the terminal to the selected session's Helpers card; **Pop Out** (⌥⌘O) moves the terminal into a window of its own; and the last shows and hides the right sidebar (⌥⌘B).
- **Pop out.** **Pop Out** in the toolbar, **View > Pop Out Terminal** (⌥⌘O) or a card's **Open in Ghostty** moves the terminal out of Irie's window into a Ghostty window of its own, titled Irie terminal. The same button, then **Pop Back In** (**View > Pop Terminal Back In**), brings it back, as do **Pop Back In** on the note left in the terminal's frame and closing the Ghostty window; **Show Window** on the note brings that window to the front. The Ghostty window shows the session you pick in the sidebar, as the terminal did, and the conversation (⌥⌘T) still shows in Irie's window. It opens at the terminal's size in cells, in the terminal's colours and text size, so popping out resizes no agent while the window fits on the screen, and nothing asks before it closes, since closing it ends only a tmux client, never an agent. While the terminal is out, Irie's own terminal is gone, so only the Ghostty window sizes the agents (§2), and a session with teammates shows alone. A pop out outlives the app: when Irie.app starts again, as after an install, it finds its Ghostty still open and stays popped out. Popping out needs Ghostty; without it the app says how to attach from any terminal. A file dropped on Irie's window while the terminal is out goes nowhere: drop it on the Ghostty window.
- **Dock**, a card of the panes beside a session's terminal (below), with the controls you keep an eye on, as cmux's Dock has them: live terminals that each run one command, such as `lazygit`, a log or a test watcher, and web pages, such as the one your app serves (DESIGN §2.3, §2.4). A repo lists its controls in `.irie/dock.json`, which a team commits, or in cmux's `.cmux/dock.json`, which works unchanged; your own, for every session, are in `dock.json` in Irie's config folder (**Open My Dock File** writes a first one). cmux's own personal file is not read. A control of the repo runs in the shown session's worktree, so a git view shows that agent's own branch; each session gets its copy the first time its Dock shows, and keeps it until the session closes. Controls stack top to bottom in the file's order, at the height the file asks or sharing what is left; each has a header with its title and what it runs, ⟳ to restart it, and ■ to stop it, which keeps it stopped until you start it (▶). A terminal control is a live terminal on Irie's dock server, so a click types to it and it never resizes an agent; one whose command ended keeps its last screen. A web page shows in a web view that keeps no data and loads only from its page's own origin; a link elsewhere and any new window open in your browser, ⟳ reloads it and ↗ opens it there. A repo's file runs nothing until you trust it: the card shows what it would run, with **Trust and Run** and **Not Now**, and it asks again whenever the file changes, so an agent's edit never runs before you look. An empty Dock offers **Set Up My Dock…**, which starts an agent in the session's repo that reads it, asks you what to watch when that is unclear, and writes and commits `.irie/dock.json`.
- **A session's helpers**, its **Helpers** card in the panes beside its terminal. A click on a helper's line under its card, or on its chip in the conversation, or **View > Show Session Helpers** (⇧⌘H), turns the panes to the card, open on that helper, beside the terminal or the conversation, whichever shows (DESIGN §4.4, docs/proposals/split-pane.md). A helper has no terminal of its own, so the app draws it from its transcript. The card's top is a strip of the session's helpers, one pill each, in the order they started: a ring while one runs, a check once it is done, red while it needs you. A finished helper keeps its pill until **Drop** takes it off. Under the strip are the helper's kind and task, how long it has run, and what it does now. Then comes its conversation: the task from the session, what it says, and its calls in plain words. **Settings > Appearance > Helpers** picks how it looks (`ui.helper_style`): **Browser**, cards like the conversation's, or **Terminal**, a terminal's lines on its dark ground. The terminal never splits for a helper, since a helper has no terminal. A helper can't take messages from Irie yet; in the terminal, Claude Code's own panel can type to it.
- **Your helpers**, the right sidebar's **Helpers** tab (⌥⌘3): programs of yours that Irie keeps running for you, such as a listener for what you text yourself, each from a `[helpers.NAME]` table of a manifest in the plugins folder (DESIGN §4.17). Each shows how it stands (running since when, starting, off, or what is wrong, in red), the line its status file gives, and what it works on: a row of a session shows that session, and a row of a file opens it, a text file in a code card beside the session on show. ⟳ starts it again, ↗ opens its folder or file, and its switch turns its plugin on or off. A manifest whose helper runs a program is off until you turn it on.
- **Review**, the right sidebar's tab for what is ready for you to look at: the sessions to verify, finished and not parked, in the list's order, each with what its card says, such as “Not through: 2 commits not in live”; then, under `spawn.close_when_done`, those that closed on their own in the last day, as **Ready to review** lists them. A row joins at the end and leaves only when you act on it, so nothing slides under the pointer. A click shows a session, or reopens one that closed; its Ⓟ parks a finished one, and × drops a closed one from both lists (it stays in history).
- **Notices**, the right sidebar's tab that keeps what the app told you: every notice, newest first, errors in red, each with its time. The app keeps the newest 100 across launches, and **Clear** empties the list. The notice on show sits at the top right of the working area, never over the task bar. It goes by itself, a confirmation after a few seconds and an error after 8 at least, but stays while the pointer rests on it, and its × closes it at once. Errors you have not seen yet put a red count on the tab.
- **A session beside its teammates.** With **Settings > General > Agent teams** on (`spawn.teammates = "panes"`), the Claude Code sessions started from then on may start agent teams, and each teammate is a Claude Code session of its own in a terminal (DESIGN §2.3). While the terminal shows a session that has teammates, it splits as cmux does: the session's own terminal on the left, and its teammates' in a column on the right, one under another at equal heights. Each has a header, a dot and its name in the colour Claude Code gave it, its agent type, and whether it works, waits or is done. Each is live: a click puts the keyboard in it, and the wheel scrolls its history. The divider between the session and the column can be dragged, and the app keeps where it was left; a window under 640 points puts the column under the session. The session's own terminal there is a client of a view of its window alone, so only that agent takes the split's size, and every other agent keeps its own. Its card lists the teammates under its helpers, and a click on one shows the split. With Ghostty drawing the terminals, Ghostty's split keys move between the panes, move the bar, and zoom one pane to fill the split; a zoomed pane only grows over the others, so no terminal ends for it (§4). Pausing the session holds its teammates at their next tool call. When the last teammate ends, the session's terminal is whole again.
- **Panes, a rolodex beside the terminal** ([workspaces.md](proposals/workspaces.md) §4, `Rolodex.swift`). **View > Show Panes** (⌥⌘P) or the toolbar's stack button slides in a wheel of cards between the terminal, or the conversation, and the right sidebar; the terminal makes room once it is in, so the shown agent resizes once, as for the right sidebar. Each card is a pane of your own: **Terminal**, a login shell in the session's folder, which the core runs on the Dock's tmux server (the `pane` op), so it never resizes an agent, outlives a restart and ends with its session, and whose last screen stays with **New Shell** when it exits; beside a session on another machine it is ssh to that machine, by its `[peers.NAME] ssh` name, and a login shell there in the session's folder; **Browser**, an address bar over a page, which takes an address, a port such as `3000` for your own app, or words to search, keeps its page as the wheel turns, and keeps no data once the app quits; **Code**, a light editor of the session's files (below); **Helpers**, the session's own helpers, and **Dock**, its controls (both above); and a plugin's page, such as **Markets** while that plugin is on, or **Mail**, every account's mail as the right sidebar's Mail tab shows it, while the mail plugin is on. `irie ctl card KIND [SESSION]` asks the app for a card beside a session, the one it runs in by default: the session's card of that kind, or a new one, which the wheel slides in and turns to when that session is on show, and which waits in front of its cards otherwise; the keyboard stays where it was. An agent asked to open your mail beside it runs `irie ctl card mail`; the app alone reads the mail. Each session keeps its own cards across launches, and whether they show and are folded is its own too, as it last had them, while the right sidebar is the same for every session: going to a session shows its cards, or not, at once, and how wide they are is one choice for every session. The terminal is narrower beside a session's cards than without, so the app tells the core the size it shows each session's terminal at, in cells (the `sizes` op), and the core keeps each agent's window at its own session's size rather than the slot's: going between a session whose cards show and one whose don't resizes no other agent, and the core gives the slot the size of the session coming in before it swaps it in, while the terminal keeps its size until the core has (`PaneSizes.swift`). It counts the cells only for Ghostty's terminal, checked against the cells Ghostty has; while the terminal is popped out, or SwiftTerm draws it, every agent takes the slot's size as before. The cards' index tabs stand in one row along the top, level with the terminal's top: the front card's joined to its card, and wider than the others when the cards are many, so its name reads; the cards behind show as thin edges above the front card's. The front card's foot is level with the terminal's, and its pane fills it to the edge. **⌥Tab** takes the keyboard from the terminal to the first card, then to each card in turn, turning the wheel to it, and back to the terminal after the last; **⌥⇧Tab** goes the other way. A turn flips the front card over its foot toward you while the next comes up behind it, and a turn back brings the card before up over it; the wheel turns the short way round, so after the last card comes the first. A click on a tab turns to that card, spinning past those between; a scroll over the tabs turns the wheel a card a notch, with a tick on a Force Touch trackpad. The + at the row's end, **View > New Pane** or a blank card adds a card (a blank card also takes T, B, C for Code, M or E for Mail); a tab's × or its menu closes the card, and the menu moves it left or right. Drag the wheel's edge to make it wider or narrower, applied once on release, and double-click it for its first width. Beside the terminal the wheel stops where the terminal would have less than about 60 columns; drag on past that point and the edge holds there a moment, with a tick, then goes on over the terminal, up to a peek of it, while a band shows what the panes will cover. Let go there and the terminal keeps its narrowest while the wheel lies over it, like a window over another: whichever has the keyboard lies on top, so ⌥Tab, a click in a card or on a tab brings the wheel up, and the terminal or the message box taking the keyboard brings that up; while the keyboard is elsewhere, such as in the list, the last one stays on top. A click on what shows of the other brings it to the front, as a click on a window does, and nothing changes size as they swap, so no agent resizes for it. Over the terminal the wheel's tabs start where the terminal ends, so they show either way; the edge that shows between them, the wheel's or the terminal frame's, is the one to drag, and letting go short of the terminal's narrowest stands the wheel beside it again. **View > Panes Over the Terminal** does the same with a click rather than a drag, and a second click stands the wheel beside the terminal again, leaving the terminal's width as it was. **View > Fold Panes** (⇧⌥⌘P), the fold button beside the +, or a drag of the edge well past the wheel's narrowest folds the wheel down to a strip at the window's edge: the cards seen from the side, each card's tab standing on its side with its mark and name, the front card's out furthest and in its colour, and the terminal takes the room. The strip's button, ⇧⌥⌘P again, ⌥Tab, a new card, or a drag out from its edge unfolds it, and a click on a tab unfolds it at that card, which takes the keyboard. A fold gives the terminal its room back as it starts and an unfold makes room once the wheel is in, so each resizes the shown agent once; folded or not is the session's own, kept across launches, and Show Panes shows the whole wheel. A card asked for beside a session not on show (`irie ctl card`, `irie ctl open`) shows with its cards when that session does. With Reduce Motion on, a turn, a fold and an unfold each happen in one step.
- **Code cards** (`CodePane.swift`, `CodeEditor.swift`, `CodeDocument.swift`, `CodeSyntax.swift`, `CodeThemes.swift`). A code card is a light editor of the session's files, drawn by the app itself. **⌘P** or its magnifier opens a file of the session's folder by the letters of its name (git's list of the folder's files, or a walk of it outside a repository); its sidebar button shows the folder as a tree beside the file, folders first, each folding open with a click, a click on a file opening it, and the folders down to the file on show open; the files it has open stand as chips along its top, a dot on one with typing unsaved, and its foot says where the file is, the caret's line and column, the language and whether it is saved. It colours code in the theme picked in **Settings > Appearance > Code** or the card's ⋯ menu: the app's own, which takes the terminal's colours, or one of the well-known themes listed there, each following the window's light or dark look where it has both. Return keeps a line's indent, and goes one in after a line that opens a block; Tab and ⇧Tab, **⌘]** and **⌘[** indent and outdent the lines picked; **⌘/** comments them out or back in; **⌘F** finds, with **⌥⌘F** to replace and **⌘G** for the next; **⌘L** goes to a line; and **⌘W** closes the file, not the session, while the card has the keyboard, in its editor or any field of its own (the card itself when it has no file). What you type is saved a moment after the last key, and as the card, the session or the app steps back, only ever whole: through a copy put in the file's place, which keeps its permissions and links, so a save that fails, as on a full disk, leaves the file as it was. A file whose every line ends in CRLF keeps CRLF; one with mixed line ends keeps them as they are. A file deleted or moved while you type isn't written back until you press **Save It Again**. Typing that can't reach its file (a conflict waiting on the card, a file gone, a failed save) is kept in a rescue file in the app's support folder, Recovered Code, and when the file next opens, after a quit, an install or a crash, it comes back as a conflict to settle. A change on disk, as when the agent edits the file, comes in within a second while nothing typed is unsaved: only the lines that changed change, so the caret stays, they are marked in the card's colour down the gutter, and **⌘Z** takes the change back; while something typed is unsaved, a bar asks whether to keep yours or use the disk's. A right-click on lines asks the agent about them: `internal/core/pane.go:12-20` goes into its prompt and the terminal takes the keyboard. **`irie ctl open FILE[:LINE[:COL]] [SESSION]`** opens a file on the session's code card, at that line, adding a card when it has none: each Claude Code session is told of it when it starts, so asking the agent to show you some code puts it there. **`:open FILE[:LINE]`** in the task bar does the same for the selected session at once, with no agent: a relative name is the session's folder's. A session on another machine has no code card yet.
- **Links in the terminal** (`TerminalLinks.swift`, and the core's `link` op). ⌘-click a link in a terminal, the agent's, a teammate's or a Terminal card's, with either engine: an address, a file's path as the terminal shows it, perhaps with `:12` or `:12:5` after it, or a link a program wrote (OSC 8), which tmux passes on to the app's terminals. While ⌘ is held, a link under the pointer is underlined. Where it opens is **Settings > Appearance > Links in the Terminal**, `ui.terminal_links` in the config. **Panes**, the default, opens a page on a Browser card of the session's panes, a new card at the wheel's right end, or the card that shows that page already, and a file of text on the session's Code card at its line; the card comes to the front with the keyboard, as a window you open does, and a hidden or folded wheel slides in first. **Your Browser** opens them in the Mac's own apps, as before: a page in your default browser, a file in its own app. ⇧⌘-click opens a link in the other place. Either way a mail address, a folder, a picture or anything else no card shows opens in its own app, but an app, an installer or a file that would run something is picked out in Finder and never opened, since a link a program wrote can say one thing and lead to another; a relative path is the session's folder's, and a path that names no file says so. A link's click goes to neither tmux nor the agent, so in Claude Code's fullscreen view, which takes the mouse, it opens where the setting says rather than where Claude Code would; a plain click still goes to the agent. Beside a session on another machine, its pages open here and its files say they are there. The core decides what each link opens as, so the phone and Windows apps, which have no panes, keep opening links as they did.
- **Tokens per session.** Each card shows the session's token count, and the panel splits it into input, output, cache read and cache write. The core reads each row's `tokens` from the session's transcript and its finished subagents' transcripts. It counts each reply once, because a reply spans several lines that all repeat its usage.
- **Accessibility.** VoiceOver reads each card as one element: name, state, what it needs or does, when it started ("started 3 minutes ago") and its tokens. The card's actions offer what the hover buttons and the right-click menu do. Notices are announced. Each goes by itself, an error after 8 s at least, and never while the pointer rests on it; the right sidebar's **Notices** tab keeps them all, so none is lost as it goes. Text colours keep 4.5:1 contrast, with stronger ones for Increase Contrast. The selected card always shows the selection's full fill; its edge runs bolder while the list has the keyboard.
- **The shown session's conversation** on the right, by default (DESIGN §4.7). You and Claude take turns: your messages on soft cards, Claude's replies in Markdown. Each tool call is one line in plain words ("Ran the tests ✓ 40 s"), with how long it took and the lines an edit changed, and opens to its exact command or path, or a failed call to its output. Two or more reads, edits or searches in a row gather into one line ("Edited 3 files +42 −7"). Subagents are chips with their kind and task, and what they do now while they run. A session that started before Irie followed conversations says so and offers its terminal. The view follows the selection and keeps to the newest turn while you are at the bottom. A code block marked `mermaid`, as an agent in a tutor mode writes where a picture helps ([learning.md](proposals/learning.md) §4.8), is drawn as a diagram in the palette's colours, with **Show Source** for its text. Mermaid's own library (MIT), which `mac/build.sh` fetches at a pinned release and checks by its hash, draws it in a web view that loads nothing but that script: no network and no files, with Mermaid's strict security level, and the wheel still scrolls the conversation over it. Without the library, or for a diagram that does not parse, the block shows as text.
- **A message box** at the foot of the conversation types to the agent through the core, the way a person would (`send`): Return sends it, and an agent at work reads it at its next step. It shows as pending until the agent takes it. While the agent waits on you in its terminal (a permission, a question, a plan), a banner offers the terminal instead of the box, and a card at the end says what it asks, with a question's choices or the plan. Answering still happens in the terminal. Your turn in teach mode is different: the agent waits at its prompt for the part it left you at a `TODO(human)`, so the card says what to write and the box stays, for your word that it is written.
- **The shown agent's live terminal** behind the conversation, a flip away (**View > Show Terminal**, ⌥⌘T, or **Terminal** under the message box; **View > Show Conversation** flips back). It stays attached while the conversation shows, so the flip is instant; the choice is remembered. The terminal sits in a rounded frame edged in the colour round that session's card: red while it needs you, green once it is done, its own colour while it works, and sun otherwise. Two lines in the same colour, a little apart, run straight across from the middle of the card's trailing edge to the frame. Each turns into both edges on a curve, the upper line up and the lower one down, like a U over an upside-down U, and both edges open between them, so the card and its terminal read as one outline. Between the lines the card's own colour runs on and fades into the terminal's, over the divider between the sidebar and the terminal, so nothing of the window shows through the join. They show while that stretch of the card is in sight, and narrow from the card's bolder edge while the list has the keyboard. The list brings the selected card into sight whenever the selection moves, and whenever it is drawn afresh, as at launch or for a new palette, so the card and its lines are never left below the others. Selecting a row swaps that agent in, and Return (or **Type to it**) moves the keyboard to the message box, or to the terminal while it shows. A file dropped on the terminal or the conversation puts its path in the agent's prompt, as a terminal does, and Claude Code takes an image's path as the image. From the conversation, the path goes with your next message.
- **A task bar** at the foot of the sidebar, always three lines tall; longer text scrolls inside it. It shares one panel with the **List | Repo** switch and the details panel above it, lighter than the list, with rounded top corners. Beside the text, a menu picks where a task runs: a repo the `@` words know (a new worktree of it), a folder chosen with **Choose a Folder…**, or **Not in a repo** (a new scratch folder). A click or ⌃1–9 shows one, with the card you showed there last; **+** makes one and lets you name it at once; a chip's menu renames it, moves it left or right, or removes it, which puts its cards in the first space. Each space has its own Pinned and Parked, while every paired machine's group and Outside Irie show in each. **Move** in a card's menu (To Irie, To Steak, To New Space), ⌃⌥1–9, or a drop on a chip puts the card, or every card picked with it, in another space, as one step that ⌘Z takes back. A group of yours goes whole, with every card the core has in it then: **Move “Steak” to Space** in its header's menu lists the other spaces and **New Space**, and its header dragged onto a chip moves it there. While a drag holds a card or a group, **+** reads **New Space**, and what is dropped on it goes to a new space, a group to one named after it, which the sidebar then shows; a card's new space opens its name to edit. A chip lights only for a drop that would move something, so not the space on show. A task you type starts in the space on show, and a session an agent starts goes in that agent's space. The title bar's counts count every space, and a card shown from elsewhere, such as a count or a notice, takes the sidebar to its space. If macOS uses ⌃1–9 to switch desktops, the chips still work.
- **Sub tabs.** Under the pointer, a helper's or teammate's row under a card shows a ×, which hides it from the card while it keeps working; "2 hidden · Show" brings them back. Its menu has **Stop Helper** or **Stop Teammate** (`stop_sub`): a helper ends at its next step, its tool calls turned away with word to end, and a teammate's terminal ends.
- **Close from under the pointer.** On the card under the pointer, a × stands in for its mark, as on a browser's tab, and a middle click closes the card too. **Settings > Tabs** turns both off.
- **Drag to reorder.** Drag a card, or every card picked with it, and it goes where it is dropped. A line with a dot at its head shows where: between the two rows the pointer is between, as far in as the group it would join. Over the top half of a row the drop goes just before it, over the bottom half just after it, so a card lands between any two rows, under the last card and beside a card's helpers too. Over a group's header, past its top edge, the drop goes inside the group, first in it when the group is open and last when it is folded; the header lights, its rail with it, and held there for a moment a folded group springs open, as a folder does in Finder, so the drop can aim inside it. Where a group ends, the drop can go last in the group or after it, in the group around it, out to the top of the list: the row the pointer is on decides, the group's last card for inside and the row after for outside, and moving the pointer sideways picks among them, each 24 points to the left a group further out and to the right a group further in, as a file dragged sideways in Finder's list view picks the folder it lands in. Under the last card the drop goes to the top of the list, last, or, moved right, last in the groups that end there. What is held fades meanwhile, and a drop that changes nothing shows nothing. Hold it near the list's top or bottom edge and the list scrolls that way, so it can go past the cards out of sight: slowly at first, about two cards' height (a fifth of the list) in from the edge, and faster the nearer the edge, and at its fastest over the find field above the list. It eases into each new speed and out of it, so it never lurches as the pointer moves. A card taken up near an edge waits for the pointer to move before that edge scrolls. A group dragged by its header does the same (`DragScroll.swift`). A card dropped among the parked cards, or on Parked's header, is parked there, and one dropped among the pinned cards, or on Pinned's header, is pinned there; a parked or pinned card dropped among the others comes back there, in the Repo view among its own repo's cards. **Move > Up** and **Move > Down** in its menu, and among VoiceOver's actions, move it within its group without a drag, and **Session > Move** does it from the keyboard: ⌥⌘↑ and ⌥⌘↓, and with ⇧ to the top or bottom of its group. The order is the core's (`reorder`), so the terminal sidebar shows it too, and it survives restarts. Neither a state change nor a boost ever moves a card (`Drops.swift`, `DropModel.swift`).
- **Right-click on a row,** or its ⋯ button: show and type to it, open it in Ghostty (which pops the terminal out, or brings its window to the front), rename it, pause or resume it, boost it, park or unpark it, move it up or down, show the folder in Finder, copy the folder path, branch name or session ID, and close it.
- **Renaming a session.** Double-click its card, or choose **Rename…** from the card's ⋯ or right-click menu or **Session > Rename…**, and the name opens to edit in place, at its own size, so the card keeps its height. Return renames the session, Esc keeps the name, and clicking elsewhere renames it, as in Finder. A name can be any text: the core makes runs of spaces one space and numbers a name another session has (`rename`, DESIGN §2.4). The name is the core's, so it stays through restarts of the app and the core, and through installs. VoiceOver offers **Rename** among the card's actions.
- **Sessions close like tabs.** **File > Close** (⌘W), which names the session, as Close “api-fix”, or **Close** in a row's menu, closes it at once, without asking, and the next session in the list takes its place. The core swaps its pane out, ends its agent gently and removes its row; its folder stays, and its worktree too unless its work has landed, which only its branch then holds (DESIGN §4.5). A session you close is done: the jot dog lets it be, and **Ready to review** lists only the sessions that closed on their own. **File > Reopen** (⇧⌘T), which names the session it brings back, is the undo: the session comes back in its place in the list, carrying its conversation on.
- **Undo and Redo.** **Edit > Undo** (⌘Z) and **Redo** (⇧⌘Z) step through the core's one history, which the terminal sidebar's `u` and `U` share (DESIGN §4.5): a park or an unpark, a close, a reopen, a start, a pause or a resume. The menu names the step, as **Undo Park “api-fix”**, and a notice says what was done. Pausing or resuming them all at once, as the terminal sidebar's `F` does, is one step that names its sessions, as **Undo Pause “api-fix”, “parser” and “site”**; past four it names three and says how many more. The keys ask first, as they show nothing of the step they would take: ⌘Z shows **Undo Close “api-fix”?** in a sheet on the window, with when the step was taken (**Done 12 minutes ago. Redo does it again.**), and for a reopen whose cache has run out, what its next turn costs. Return, or the same key again, goes ahead; Esc leaves everything as it is. The core asks the question and ties it to the step, so a step that comes in while it is on show, as an agent's start or close, is never the one taken: the answer then changes nothing and says to press ⌘Z again. **Don’t ask again** in it, or **Settings > Tabs > Undo**, turns the question off. **Edit > Undo** clicked, and a notice's **Undo**, name their step already, so they take it at once. When a step is uncertain, such as closing a session that is still at work, nothing changes until you answer the core's question in an alert, headed by the step, its recommended answer first; that question is the only one a key asks then. While a text field has the keyboard and has typing to take back, ⌘Z undoes the typing instead.
- **Outside Irie.** Claude Code sessions that run in other terminals or in the background follow the cards, under a header with their count (DESIGN §4.6). Each is one line, in the order it started, with the mark of the state it matches, so a state change never moves or resizes it. A click or the arrow keys select one, and the details panel shows what it is doing, where it runs and its folder, with **Move into Irie** and **Copy Session ID**. While the session is busy or waits for you, the button is off and the panel says why. A move asks first, because the session's copy outside Irie ends, and anything it runs in the background stops. Then the conversation carries on in an Irie pane, and its terminal takes the keyboard. Right-click a row for the same, and to show or copy its folder. The keys that show a session skip these rows, since Irie can't show them.
- **Raffi's restarts (the freeze dog).** When the app stops answering, the core force quits it and opens it again, so a frozen window comes back by itself; Raffi (Raphael, DESIGN §4.11) does the same for a sidebar, and for the core itself. From the moment it has launched, a thread of the app's own (`FreezeDog.swift`) keeps a connection to the core, which pings it once a second; each ping waits for the main thread, which answers with whether the app is in front or hidden. A ping left unanswered for 10 s (**Settings > Arch Agents**, Raffi's `ui.freeze_dog`; off at `0s`, or with Raffi off) brings the restart: `sample` writes down where the app was stuck, in a report in `freezes/` beside `core.log` (the newest ten are kept), the app is force quit once its stacks are read, about 1.3 s later, and it opens again as it stood, in front, behind or hidden; while macOS still takes the old app for running, Raffi tries again for a few seconds. A notice then says how long it had not answered, and that ❤️‍🩹 Raffi restarted it. Agents keep running, as with any restart of the app (DESIGN §2.1, principle 6). The dog leaves an app under a debugger be, waits while the core itself is held up, and restarts the app three times in ten minutes at most (DESIGN §2.4).

## 2. How the terminal gets into the window

The app adds no state of its own to the core. It uses two additions to the socket API (DESIGN §2.4):

- `subscribe` with `view: "app"`. While at least one such subscription is open, the core keeps the slot zoomed (`resize-pane -Z`), so the slot fills `irie:0`. Swaps keep the zoom (`swap-pane -Z`, `select-pane -Z`). Agent windows take the zoomed slot's size, so a swap still never resizes an agent. When the last app view closes, the zoom ends and the sidebar is back. The frames wait in an inbox for the main thread, which applies every one that came in a single turn, and sets nothing in the store that did not change: a set is news to every view that read the value, even to what it was. So an upsert whose only change is a figure the app does not show, such as a session's memory, draws nothing. The store is observed value by value (Swift's Observation): a view draws again only when a value it read has changed. Views that want one row or a count read what the store works out once a change, the selected session's row, the title bar's counts and whether there are any rows, so another session's news leaves them alone. A card draws again only when its own row, its selection, its place at its group's ends or its age label changes, and only the card under the pointer has its buttons and menu. So an agent's new detail redraws its card, not the window: with 30 sessions, about 9 ms of the main thread instead of 32, and a click about 26 ms instead of 64, measured offscreen.
- `attach-info`, which returns the tmux binary, the server's socket path and the session to attach to.

The app runs `tmux -S <socket> attach-session -t irie` inside its terminal view. Because the slot is zoomed, that client shows only the shown agent, and `show` swaps agents in place.

The slot is the core's, so more than the app moves it: the terminal sidebar, `irie ctl show`, a core that started again. The selected card follows the session the core's rows say the slot shows (`shown`), except while one the app asked for is on its way, so keys that outrun the core never send the selection back to a card they passed. A `show` that fails, as against a restarting core, puts the selection back on the session the slot still shows. So the selected card, the frame round the terminal and the lines between them always name the session in the terminal. The lines' two ends are AppKit views in the selected card and the frame (`TabLink.swift`), which read where they are in the window whenever they, a view round them or the list's scroll moves; one view over the window's content draws the lines then, and only while both ends name the same session. They used to come as SwiftUI preferences across the split view's columns, which arrive in no promised order: in a test program, a card the list made after a scroll never reported where it was. The list knows each card by its group and its session together (`CardPlace` in `SidebarView.swift`), so a card that a pin or a park takes to another group is made afresh there, and the list scrolls to the selected card when it moves. Known by its session alone, a card pinned or parked while the window was open kept the view the lazy list had made for it in the group it left, which was never brought up to date: it never showed the selection, nor the lines from it.

Every resize of that client resizes the slot, and with it the shown agent and every agent window the core sizes to the slot. So the terminal changes size once for each change of the window's layout, never at each step of one. The right sidebar's edge applies on release (§1). While the sidebar's edge or the window's own is dragged, the terminal keeps the size it had, drawn from its top left in a frame that follows the drag, and takes the new size as the button comes up (`ResizeHold.swift`; a divider's drag runs its own event loop, so the app looks at the button rather than waiting for its release).

Only one client sizes the slot at a time. tmux gives the slot the size of its latest client (`window-size latest`), so two clients of different sizes would take turns: each key typed in one after the other would resize every agent. While the terminal is popped out (§1), Irie's own client is gone and the Ghostty window's is the only one. Popping back in attaches Irie's again and detaches only the Ghostty window's client, the one whose process descends from that Ghostty, which Ghostty started with `-e`, so Ghostty quits with its last window and the app hears that it has.

Two details of the generated tmux.conf follow from this. The `window-resized` hook leaves a zoomed window alone, because resizing the sidebar would end the zoom. In a terminal attached to the zoomed slot, the focus key goes to the agent, because there is no sidebar pane to move to. The app catches Ctrl+] itself (§4), so from the app it never reaches tmux. While the app is open, a terminal attached with `irie attach` also sees only the slot.

## 3. Build and run

`mac/build.sh` builds `mac/build/Irie.app`. It calls `swiftc` directly because SwiftPM 5.9, the version in the Command Line Tools for macOS 14.1, stops at `unable to lookup item 'PlatformPath'`. `tools/live.sh` (AGENTS.md) runs it when `mac/` or `tools/mkicon` changed, installs the app in `~/Applications`, registers it again so Finder and the Dock show its current icon, and relaunches it if it was running.

**Version and build number.** The app's version (`CFBundleShortVersionString`) is the release it comes from, `internal/version`'s `Base`. Its build number (`CFBundleVersion`) decides which copy opens when macOS opens the app by its bundle identifier rather than its path: System Settings does so to reopen the app after you change one of its privacy settings, and so does `open -b`. Launch Services then takes the copy with the highest build number, and among equal ones any copy it knows, such as an old build left in a worktree, which then runs without the features made since. So a build to install (`IRIE_MAC_INSTALL=1`, as `tools/live.sh` and `tools/release.sh` build it) takes the UTC time it was built, as `20261114.153012`, and the newest install wins; every other build takes `0`, so it never wins over an install.

The icon is Irie's mark, a warm sun rising over two calm waves on a deep-green rounded square (DESIGN §7.2). `go run ./tools/mkicon` draws it with Go's standard library and writes the ten PNGs of an iconset, and the script packs them into `Irie.icns` with `iconutil`, so the build needs Go as well. macOS draws an icns as it is, so the icon carries its own shape: an 824-pixel square with continuous corners in the middle of the 1024-pixel canvas, on a soft shadow. Below 64 pixels the rays would blur into the sun, so the small sizes draw a larger sun over two level lines, with their edges on whole pixels. The output is the same on every run, which its tests check.

The terminals are drawn by Ghostty's own engine, GhosttyKit from [Ghostty](https://github.com/ghostty-org/ghostty) v1.3.1 (MIT), when it builds, and else by [SwiftTerm](https://github.com/migueldeicaza/SwiftTerm) v1.18.0 (MIT); **Settings > Appearance > Terminal drawn by** switches between them. Either way each terminal is a tmux client of one target, so tmux and the core see no difference. `mac/ghostty.sh` builds GhosttyKit once per release into the same cache as SwiftTerm, as `ghostty-<release>-<arch>`: it fetches Zig 0.15.2, the one Zig that builds Ghostty 1.3, and Ghostty's source, refusing either unless its SHA-256 matches; it fetches Ghostty's dependencies with curl, since Zig's own fetch stalled on their mirror, and files each in Zig's cache, where the build checks it against the hash its `build.zig.zon` names; it patches one constant the macOS 14 SDK lacks (`kCVPixelFormatType_30RGB_r210`, which Ghostty declares and never uses); and it builds with Xcode's Metal compiler, from Xcode as `xcode-select` names it or else `/Applications/Xcode.app`. The first build takes a few minutes. Ghostty's terminfo and shell integration go into the app's resources, where it finds them. Without Xcode, or without a network on that first build, the app is built with SwiftTerm alone, and `IRIE_MAC_NO_GHOSTTY=1` leaves Ghostty out. Ghostty reads your own Ghostty config (font, colours, keys), and Irie's terminal ground and ink go on top, so the terminal matches its frame, with the selection's colours that go with them. Each terminal's tmux client starts a tenth of a second after its surface takes its size, so a client never starts at another size, which for the main terminal would resize the slot and every agent with it.

A drag in a terminal selects there, not in tmux. tmux takes the mouse in every terminal (`mouse on`, for its wheel and its clicks) and hands it on to a program that asks for it, as Claude Code's fullscreen view does, so a drag would otherwise select inside that program, with a round trip through tmux for every move, or in tmux's copy mode, which drops the selection and jumps back to the bottom when the button comes up. While the left button is down the terminal reports nothing to tmux: Ghostty's `toggle_mouse_reporting` turns its reporting off for that one surface, and SwiftTerm's `allowMouseReporting` does the same, until the button comes up. The press itself waits, so a press that never moves 4 points goes to tmux as a click when the button comes up, where it went down. A double- or triple-click's first click goes to tmux that way, and Ghostty is given the presses it did not count, so it counts the next one as AppKit does. The app copies the selection itself, as Ghostty's own ⌘C does (Ghostty's `copy-on-select` is off), so the setting applies at once. SwiftTerm drops its selection at any output while it reports the mouse, and tmux draws all the time, so there a finished selection holds with reporting still off, and the next click, key or wheel turns it back on first. The terminal's own menu, on a right-click or a Control-click, means neither click reaches tmux, whose right-click menu (for a program that does not take the mouse) holds Kill, and whose Control-click swaps panes.

SwiftTerm is v1.18.0, the newest release whose manifest builds with Swift 5.9. The script fetches it, refuses the download unless its SHA-256 matches, and compiles it once into a static library in `~/Library/Caches/irie` (or `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/irie`), named for the release, the target and the compiler. Every checkout and worktree shares it, so a fresh worktree's build compiles only Irie's own files. SwiftTerm draws with Metal: its renderer compiles `Shaders.metal`, which the script copies into the app, when the app runs, so no Xcode is needed. Where Metal is missing it draws with Core Graphics.

`Irie --snapshot DIR` draws the window offscreen from fixture sessions in `/home/alice`, never a core. It writes a PNG of each palette in light and dark mode at the default size, such as `rosewater-light.png`; `window-narrow.png` and `window-wide.png` at 820 and 1500 points; `details-minimized.png` and `details-tall.png`, with the details panel minimized and dragged as tall as it goes; each tab of the Settings window in both modes, from a fixture of what the `config` op answers, such as `settings-agents-dark.png`; and Make a Theme's pages, `make-theme-colours-light.png`, `make-theme-colours-dark.png` and `make-theme-layouts-light.png`, and `make-theme-change-light.png` with the fixture's own theme open to change and every colour shown, with a theme and layouts of the fixture's own in place of the config folder's. It writes them to DIR, prints the title bar's height and where the first card starts, in points, and exits. It shows the terminal view, whichever view the app has saved, with an empty terminal in the frame, so the frame and the lines from the selected card show. It is how a change to the views is checked without a screen. The window has to be ordered in for SwiftUI to lay it out, and macOS brings a titled window onto a screen however far off it was placed, so it is see-through, lets clicks pass and goes to a desktop the user is not looking at before it is ordered in: it never shows, and the app takes no focus ("Out of sight", below). The picture is drawn with `cacheDisplay`, which gives a fully rounded edge a short straight piece at each end that the screen does not show.

`Irie --render-conversation DIR` draws the conversation view offscreen with SwiftUI's ImageRenderer, from fixture items in `/home/alice`, never a core: `conversation-<palette>-<light|dark>.png` for each palette, and `waiting-…png` for a session that waits on you. ImageRenderer leaves out AppKit's own controls, such as the text field, so the rendering draws the field's placeholder in its place; `--snapshot` shows the same fixtures in the real window.

`Irie --record OUT.mp4 --reel FILE --socket PATH` films the window while a reel types tasks, moves between cards, answers an agent in its terminal and closes and opens the window, then exits (docs/demo/README.md, "Mac app recordings"). The window has to show to be filmed, so it opens on a desktop the user is not looking at and lets clicks pass, and the app never takes the focus; when the user can see every desktop it does not film at all, rather than play the reel over their work. Each frame is the window read back from the window server with `CGWindowListCreateImage`, its terminal and animations included, which macOS allows an app for its own windows without the Screen Recording permission; the frames go to an H.264 MP4 at 30 frames a second, in sRGB. A reel types through the defaults that hold the task bar's text, so `--record` runs only as a copy whose bundle id ends in `.recording`, which `docs/demo/record.sh` makes, and never touches the installed app's defaults or the user's own Irie.

**Signing.** The script signs the app ad hoc, which runs on this Mac only, unless `IRIE_SIGN_IDENTITY`, or `git config irie.sign`, names a code signing identity in the keychain, as `security find-identity -v -p codesigning` lists them. An "Apple Development" identity, which Xcode makes once you sign in with your Apple ID (Xcode > Settings > Accounts), signs it as yours; a "Developer ID Application" identity, from the paid Apple Developer Program, signs it for other Macs, with the hardened runtime and a secure timestamp from Apple, as notarization wants. A new identity is a new app to macOS's privacy settings, which then ask again for what they had allowed the ad hoc one. `tools/live.sh` signs the `irie` it installs with the same identity, so macOS's firewall keeps its answer to whether `irie`, the phone app's gateway, may accept incoming connections from one install to the next (PHONE.md §3).

`IRIE_MAC_UNIVERSAL=1 mac/build.sh`, on Apple silicon, adds an Intel slice to the app, as a release's app has (`tools/release.sh`). That slice draws its terminals with SwiftTerm, because GhosttyKit builds for the Mac's own processor only, and `lipo` joins the two.

`IRIE_MAC_NO_SWIFTTERM=1 mac/build.sh` builds with Apple's frameworks only. The terminal then opens in a Ghostty window that follows the selected session. Where SwiftPM works (Xcode, or newer Command Line Tools), `mac/Package.swift` builds the same app.

The app finds the core the way `irie` does (`internal/paths`). `--socket PATH`, or `IRIE_CORE_SOCKET`, points it at another Irie. It needs a core with `attach-info`; an older one gets a message to run the new build's `irie up`.

With no core answering when it opens, the app starts Irie itself, once a launch and only before it has reached a core, so a core that restarts while the app is open is the supervisor's to bring back. It runs `irie up --login-env`, which starts the server with the login shell's environment (DESIGN §7.2), so agents find `claude`, `git` and `node` as a terminal would. The irie it runs is the first of `$XDG_DATA_HOME/irie/bin/irie`, where the installer and `tools/live.sh` put it, `~/.local/bin/irie`, Homebrew's folders and `~/go/bin/irie`; else its own copy, `Contents/Helpers/irie`, which a release's app carries (`tools/release.sh`). That copy goes to `$XDG_DATA_HOME/irie/bin` first, without a download's quarantine, so agents keep a path that lasts when the app moves. When irie fails, the sidebar shows what it said, with **Set Up Irie…**, which opens Terminal on `irie setup` through a `.command` file, needing no permission to script Terminal, and **Try Again**. With no irie at all, it shows the installer's line and a **Copy** button. Later, offline, its **Start Irie** button does the same.

`Irie --bench` measures what one change to the store costs the window: the main thread's CPU time for one session's update, a click on a card, a notice, and every row changing, with 30 fixture sessions drawn offscreen, never a core. Each figure is the CPU of a run of changes less the same run with none. A change that draws more than it shows costs here first; with 30 sessions one session's update cost about 9 ms, against 32 before cards drew only on their own changes. Like `--snapshot`'s, its window never shows, and it takes no focus. In an agent's shell, start it with `open -g -n -W --stdout FILE -a Irie.app --args --background --bench`.

`Irie --render-onboarding DIR` draws what a first run shows, offscreen with ImageRenderer from fixtures, in light and dark mode: the welcome, ready, signed out and with the first task in the task bar; the banner; the first task's card at each step; and the sidebar starting Irie, failing to, with no irie, and offline.

**Hot loading.** `tools/hot.sh` keeps a preview of a checkout's app open beside Irie.app while you change it, and shows each change a few seconds after the save, with nothing installed. `mac/build.sh --hot OBJ APP` builds the preview unoptimized and incremental: the Swift driver's output file map names each file's object and dependencies in OBJ, so a build after an edit compiles only the files the edit reaches, then links. An edit to one view's body costs about 6 s of processor time, against about 35 s for a clean unoptimized build and minutes for the app's optimized one. The preview is **Irie Preview**, with a bundle identifier of its own for each checkout, so it has its own Dock icon and keeps its own window and choices; its first window takes the app's choices but not its frame. Builds take turns between two slots, so a new build never writes over the one that runs. Each opens with `--away --preview NAME --hot DIR --hot-pid PID --replace PID`, keeps its window out of sight until it has drawn the sessions, puts it over the old build's window, in its frame and on its desktop, and ends the old build. The app adds no state of its own, so nothing is lost but the scroll, and the window seems to change in place. A build that fails leaves the shown one up. The preview's phrase bar names the checkout and says when a build runs, when it finished, or what failed, in red, with the whole error on hover.

A preview is a client of the running core, as the app is: its sessions are the real ones, and what you do in it happens to them. A change that needs a new op shows once a core that has it is live. It differs from the app in four ways. Every tmux client it starts attaches with `-f ignore-size`, so while any other client is attached it never sizes a window, the slot and every agent included (DESIGN §2.1, principle 6). It never sends `theme`, so the agents' colours stay the app's. The freeze dog only force quits a stuck preview, since one opened again would lack hot.sh's arguments. And it quits when hot.sh ends, however it ends. It opens on a desktop you are not looking at, never the one you work on, and takes no focus (DESIGN §4.11, "Out of sight"); a new build takes the old one's place on whichever desktop it is, so you watch it change by going to that desktop, and keeps the focus the old one had. `tools/hot.sh --snapshot DIR` also draws the fixture after each build, `--no-open` builds without a window, and `--once` builds once. Closing the preview stops hot.sh; one that crashes, or that the freeze dog stops, opens again with the next build. What the preview prints shows in hot.sh's output.

`mac/snapshot.sh DIR` runs all three drawings into DIR from the preview's build: `mac/build.sh --hot`, with its objects kept in `mac/build/snapshot`, so a run after an edit compiles only the files it reaches. It is how to see a change to the views as pictures before it is committed. It installs nothing, shows no window and takes no focus; `tools/live.sh` still builds the real app.

**Out of sight.** A window the app opens on its own never lands on a desktop the user can see, not even behind the app in front (DESIGN §4.11). macOS has no public interface to desktops, so `Desktops.swift` calls SkyLight, the window server's private framework, by name as the app runs, as yabai does: it reads each display's desktops and the one each shows, and moves a window to another before the window server first shows it, with the call each macOS takes (a tag the desktop wears for a moment on 12.7, 13.6, 14.5 and from 15, the plain move before those), then checks that the window is there. With `--away`, which hot.sh passes, the window opens on a desktop out of sight: the one it was on last while that one still is, else the display's last; a first preview then waits behind whatever is on that desktop. Where every desktop is in sight, or macOS will not move it, the preview hides, and its Dock icon brings it back. With `--background`, which `tools/live.sh`, the core's swap and the freeze dog pass when they open the app again, it goes back to the desktop it was on, which the app keeps in its defaults (`windowDesktop`) as the window moves; where it remembers none, or macOS will not move it, it stays where macOS puts it, behind the app in front, since hiding the user's own app would lose it. `--snapshot` and `--bench` draw in a see-through window that lets clicks pass, on another desktop when there is one; `--record` needs one. Not yet: macOS 26's own move operation, which SkyLight does not export, so where macOS 26 refuses the older calls a preview hides and the app stays where macOS puts it.

`irie away [--for 10s] [--app NAME]... -- COMMAND` does the same for anything an agent must open. It runs the app's own executable with `--away-helper` (Away.swift), which runs COMMAND and, while it runs and for `--for` after, sends each window that appears on a desktop in sight to one out of sight, when it belongs to a process COMMAND started, to an app COMMAND opens with `open` (`-a`, `-b`, `-R` for the Finder, or the app macOS gives a file or URL, also inside `sh -c`), or to an app named with `--app`; a name with a slash matches that path alone. An app COMMAND started that cannot move is hidden. Every window open before the helper ran is left alone, and so is a new window of an app that ran before once the user brings that app to the front: such a window waits a quarter of a second first, for a link the user clicked. Without the process table it counts no app as new, and without the window list it refuses to run. It says on standard error where each window went and exits with COMMAND's status, or 3 when a window stayed in sight. It takes only an app whose Info.plist has `AwayHelperVersion`, the installed one or the one `IRIE_APP` names, since an older app would open its own window instead. The helper makes no NSApplication, so Launch Services never counts it as a second copy of the app, and `tools/live.sh` and the core's swap leave it running when they stop the app.

**Where the keyboard goes.** One place holds it at a time, and it never sits where nothing shows. An audit on 2026-10-31 walked every place that takes it (the session list, the find field, a name being edited, the task bar, the message box, the main terminal, the split's panes, the new-task sheet and Settings) and fixed what trapped it:

- **A covered terminal never has the keyboard.** The terminal stays attached under the conversation and under a zoomed pane, so ⌥⌘T flips at once, but typing into it there would go to an agent nobody sees. A Ghostty terminal that does not show refuses the keyboard, and either engine lets go of it once covered, unless what covers it took it first, as the message box does. Moving the keyboard between the split's panes while one is zoomed unzooms first.
- **A plain key never acts on the whole window.** The banner of a session that waits for you had Return as its button's shortcut, and SwiftUI looks for a window's shortcuts before the field with the keyboard sees the key: whenever such a session showed, Return in the session list opened its terminal instead, and Return in the task bar, the find field or a name being edited could too. Return opens the terminal only while the banner has the keyboard. Every other shortcut has ⌘ or Ctrl in it.
- **Ctrl+] always has somewhere to go.** It found nothing while the session on show waited, had ended, or was starting, since the message box was gone or disabled, so the keyboard stayed put. The banner takes it now, and the message box takes it while the agent starts and says to wait if you send.
- **The menus stay put.** The menu bar's commands read the store, which changes several times a second while agents work, so SwiftUI rebuilt every menu that often: an open menu flickered, lost the item under the pointer or closed. The menus now read their own small set of facts (MenuBar.swift), which changes only when an item's title or state does. The window draws again only on a frame that changes something it shows; most frames carried only an agent's memory or cost, which the app does not read. While any menu is open (the menu bar's, a card's ⋯ or right-click menu, the task bar's repo menu), or a drag or a resize is under way, frames wait and go in once it ends, so nothing is rebuilt under the pointer.

**A busy machine never holds a click.** On 2026-10-31, with 35 agents at work and the machine swapping, `sample(1)` found the app's main thread drawing the window about 83% of the time, and macOS's resource reports had it at 56% of a CPU: each frame from the core, and each update of the shown conversation, drew the window again, and on a swapping machine every drawing takes longer. A click or a key waits for the drawing under way. The frames are gathered off the main thread now and go in a batch at a time (Pace.swift): at once after a quiet spell, then at most every 100 ms, 250 ms while the header's `load` says the machine is busy, as Gabriel counts it, and 500 ms while another app is in front. A batch is drawn once, a snapshot in it drops the rows before it, and a conversation update sets only what changed (DESIGN §2.9).

The terminal itself keeps every key it is given, Tab and Esc included, since the agent needs them: Ctrl+], ⌘ keys, Ctrl+Tab and a click are the ways out, and the Go menu lists them. The one plain key the app reads first is ↓ on an agent's empty prompt, for the double ↓ (above).

## 5. States and colours

Each card's mark is its state's glyph from DESIGN §5.3, drawn: `✕` failed, `·` exited (a dim red `✕` when the exit code is not 0), `◆` frozen, `‖` paused, `●` needs you, `!` stalled, a dim `‖` pausing, `◐` working, `◉` waiting (its own turn over while its helpers or teammates still work), `◌` starting, `✓` done and `○` idle. Their colours are §5.3's roles in the palette in use: red is the palette's needs colour, cyan its frozen, yellow its paused, green its done, the default its ink and dim its muted text. Only what needs you is red, and no state is told apart by colour alone. The title bar counts sessions that need you (needs you, stalled and failed), that are working (those waiting on their helpers too), that wait for you to verify and are not yet seen, and paused and frozen ones when there are any, each with its glyph. While the trod bot is within its hours (DESIGN §4.10), a moon follows the counts: in the accent while it steps in, muted while you are still at the Mac or the plan's use stops it, and its help says which, until when, and whether it keeps the Mac awake. A card the bot sent on this shift carries its boot print beside the name, with the count. The print is bright for two minutes after each send and hops each time, so you can watch it work, and its help says when and why. In the conversation, the bot's messages are drawn as its own, under its boot print, and fold to three lines. Session > Start Trod Bot Now (or the Trod Bot tab's Start Now) starts a run at once that works while you watch, until no session has work left or the plan is as full as the tab allows; the same item then stops it.

While a session works it has a colour of its own, so a glance tells the agents apart: its mark, and a wash across its card, fullest by the mark and fading toward the trailing slot. The six colours spread round the whole colour wheel, 50° apart, leaving out 30° either side of the needs red: amber, sage, teal, sky, periwinkle and mauve. They are calm. They are worked out in OKLCH at one low chroma, so they differ by hue alone and none glares, and each mark starts at the lightness of the palette's own marks. The core gives each session its colour and the session keeps it (DESIGN §4.1). Each card's colour sits at least 100° round the wheel from the card above, as near opposite as it can, so cards side by side stand apart. A wash is the card's own lightness, a little deeper in light mode and lifted in dark mode, in the hue at a lower chroma still, as far as keeps the card's text at 4.5:1. Each mark keeps 3:1 on its wash and on the selection, going deeper or lighter only where it must. A selected card is a gradient instead, so it stands out and keeps its colour: from the selection's fill by the mark into its wash, then deeper across the trailing slot, at most halfway toward its mark's colour that edges its terminal, and only as far as keeps the slot's age, tokens and buttons, drawn in ink there, at 4.5:1. Its edge takes its mark's colour, as do the lines from it and the terminal's frame, and runs bolder while the list has the keyboard. Its text keeps 4.5:1 all along the gradient, and its mark, and so its edge, 3:1 as far as the trailing slot. Under Increase Contrast every card keeps its plain fill. A session whose own turn is over while its helpers or teammates still work keeps its colour without the wash, since it is not at work itself: its `◉` mark, its second line, which says what it waits on (the accent keeps what a jot left), the helpers and teammates under its card, and, selected, its edge, the lines and the terminal's frame. The colour follows the session away from its card too. Its helpers under the card, the details panel (its mark, its state, its lines and its edge) and the subtitle above its terminal are drawn in it, the text bright or deep enough for 4.5:1 on every ground it sits on (7:1 under Increase Contrast). A warning, a helper that needs you, and how pause and boost stand keep their own colours. The app tells the core its palette (the `theme` op, on connecting and on every change), so Irie's status line in the terminal draws the working state and the rest of its line in the same colour (DESIGN §6.3).

Before the palettes, the app did not follow §5.3:

- The title bar's counts were three identical dots, told apart by colour alone.
- Working was blue; §5.3 gives it the default colour.
- Failed and stalled were drawn as needs you, a red dot, so three states shared one glyph. The details panel called all three "Needs you" and an exit "Ended".
- An exit with an error code looked like a clean one: a grey dot instead of red.
- Starting was a blue dashed ring instead of a dim one.
- The done count also counted idle and exited sessions, which the terminal sidebar does not.
- Frozen, paused and pausing had no look at all. The core derives them from milestones 5 and 6, and they are drawn now.

With `ui.red_on_done` set, the core's header says so and the app draws done in red, as the terminal sidebar does.
