# The letter: READ ME FIRST

The letter is what the people find first. It says what to do and where everything is. It never holds a password, a PIN, an account number or a recovery code: those stay in the password manager, and the letter says so.

## How to write it with the user

1. Write the outline below to `READ-ME-FIRST.md` in the user's folder, with the user's name and today's date filled in and nothing else.
2. Go through it one section at a time. Ask what goes in each part, in plain words, and write down what they say. Skip anything that doesn't apply; never press.
3. If they tell you a secret anyway, leave it out of the file and say where it belongs: in the password manager.
4. When they are happy with it, ask them to make a secure note called **READ ME FIRST** in their password manager and paste the whole text in. Give them the steps for their password manager (`password-managers.md`).
5. Then offer to delete `READ-ME-FIRST.md`: a plain copy on disk is less safe than the one in the vault. Delete it only if they say yes. Keep a line in `switch.md` that says where the letter is.

Some people would rather print it and keep it with their will. That is fine too; say where the paper copy is in `switch.md`.

## The outline

```markdown
# READ ME FIRST

Written by [name] on [date]. If you are reading this, I may have died, or I
can't look after things myself. Thank you for helping. Take it one step at a
time; nothing here has to happen today.

If I'm alive and simply forgot to check in: call me on [phone], and tell me.

## 1. The first days

- Call first: [who, how they are related, phone]
- My doctor, and any power of attorney or health-care directive: [where]
- My will: [where it is], and my lawyer: [name, firm, phone]
- Home, pets and plants: [who looks after them, where the keys are]

## 2. People who can help

- Executor or trustee: [name, phone]
- Lawyer, accountant, financial adviser: [names, phones]
- My employer's HR, if I work: [where to call]

## 3. Papers, and where they are

- Will, power of attorney, health-care directive: [where]
- Birth and marriage certificates, passports: [where]
- Insurance (life, health, home, car): [the companies, and where the policies are]
- Pensions and retirement savings: [the companies]
- Home, mortgage, car: [where the deeds and papers are]
- A safe or a safe deposit box: [where, and who has the key]

## 4. Money

- Banks: [which ones; the details are in the password manager]
- Bills and subscriptions to stop, and ones to keep: [list]
- Loans and debts: [list]
- Taxes: [where the papers are, who does them]

## 5. My digital life

- The password manager: [which one]. Every other password is in it. You
  get in through [emergency access / its emergency kit, kept at …].
- My phone: [how to unlock it, or who knows how]
- My computers: [how to get in]
- My main email: [which address]; it resets most other accounts
- Backup codes for two-factor sign-in: in the password manager
- Photos and files: [where they are, and who should have them]
- Social media: [keep, memorialise or delete]
- Anything else: [websites, domain names, other accounts that matter]

## 6. My wishes

- [Funeral or memorial, burial or cremation, organ donation, music,
  anything else]
- Letters to people: [where they are]

## 7. How this note reached you

I set up a dead man's switch: if I don't check in for [30] days, an email
from Healthchecks.io goes to the people I chose. You may have had that email.
Its account is in the password manager; pausing its checks there stops more
emails.
```
