# How these copies were verified

Checked on 25 November 2026. Each text was fetched by script from the body or print that keeps it, never retyped, and compared word for word, by script, with at least one other copy: another transcription, another print, or the print's page images read again by text recognition. Every difference was looked at on the print's page images, or for the newest laws in the enacted text, and the file follows what the page shows. Irie's own parser, `founding.Parse`, reads every file. The work notes and scripts are kept outside the repository. The texts Common Schools shares with the charters (the Mayflower Compact, the Declaration of Independence, the Virginia Statute, the Northwest Ordinance, the Constitution and the Bill of Rights) are the charters' own files, recorded in theirs. The texts on church and state were made and checked by the separation-of-church-and-state card the same way, and its record is carried here.

## What they were checked against

- **The Massachusetts Bay laws**: two scans of Shurtleff's Records, Cornell University Library's on the Internet Archive and Google's of a second copy, each page read in its images.
- **The constitutions' clauses**: Thorpe's pages in two scans of different copies on the Internet Archive, read by text recognition and by eye, and a second print or text of each state's: the Pennsylvania State Archives' transcription (in the Wayback Machine), The State Records of North Carolina (1904), Watkins's Digest of Georgia's laws (1800) and Candler's Revolutionary Records (1908), the Vermont State Archives' text, Thorpe's own print for Massachusetts, and the Early State Papers of New Hampshire (1891).
- **The Land Ordinance**: the Library's page images of the Journals and the Internet Archive's scan of another copy, four readings in all; the ordinance as printed by order of Congress in 1785, in Wikisource's transcription of the Library's broadside; and Folwell's print of the Journals (Philadelphia, 1801).
- **The Ohio Company's terms**: the Library's page images and two more scans of the Journals; and the Journal printed by order of Congress in 1787, vol. 12, Appendix, pp. 221–222.
- **The Ohio Enabling Act**: the Library's and govinfo's scans of two copies of the Statutes at Large, each read afresh; Wikisource's proofread transcription; the Annals of Congress and Thorpe, by words only.
- **The attendance act**: the State Library's page images, the Internet Archive's reading of the same scan, the Boston Municipal Register for 1853 (pp. 47–48) and The Common School Journal of 1 August 1852 (pp. 227–228).
- **Jefferson's letter**: The Papers of Thomas Jefferson, read on Founders Online in a browser.
- **The Equal Access Act**: the Act as enacted, 98 Stat. 1302–1304, and its printed page where the text recognition was unsure.
- **The school prayer section**: the House Office of the Legislative Counsel's compilation of the ESEA as amended through Public Law 118-159 (COMPS-748).
- **Texas SB 10**: the enrolled bill's PDF, and the Fifth Circuit's quotation of the codified section in Nathan v. Alamo Heights Independent School District (21 April 2026).
- **Louisiana's Act 676**: the enrolled bill, and the codified R.S. 17:2124.
- **Arkansas's Act 573**: the district court's quotation of the poster's text in Stinson v. Fayetteville School District No. 1 (4 August 2025); the struck words read from the strike lines on the Act's pages.
- **Alabama's Act**: a second reading of the same print, its plain text against its laid-out text.

## What was found

**The Massachusetts Bay laws.** The Internet Archive's text recognition of Cornell's copy was set right against the page images of both copies, by named corrections and never by retyping: in 40 places for 1642 and 58 in 61 places for 1647; the recognition of Google's copy differs in 32 and 53 places, all slips of the machine, and its images showed which specks belong to Cornell's copy alone. Shurtleff prints no marginal heading beside either order. Other copies differ, each settled on the print: the 1642 transcription that Encyclopedia.com and Wikipedia share in 15 places (it leaves out the passage with the editor's marks for lost words, writes "(find)" for "[find]" and "employ", "townesmen" and "briefe", and dates the law "April 14"), Open Book Publishers' in 5; for 1647 the University of Massachusetts Boston's transcription in 9 (among them "ye ould" where the print has "yt ould") and Ford's New-England Primer (1897) in 2. The Book of the General Lawes and Libertyes of 1648 rewrites both orders under "Children" and "Schools" (catechising and a penalty of twenty shillings added, the two-year term dropped, the five pounds made yearly), findings of the record, not changes to the files. The files keep the print's spelling and its signs of contraction (ꝓ for pro, ꝑ for per, p̃ for par, m̄ for mm, ł for the pound mark, ꝭc͛ and ꝭcra for et cetera), set its raised letters on the line, and keep the editor's marks for words lost from the decayed manuscript (×), the word he supplied ([find]) and the record's stop (./); the order numbers in the margin, the asterisk where a manuscript page begins and the print's space before semicolons are left out. Few fonts carry ꝑ, ꝓ and ꝭ; a reader's system finds one.

**The constitutions.** Each clause is Thorpe's word for word and mark for mark in both scans. Avalon's transcription of Pennsylvania's has one slip, a capital "In", which both scans and the State Archives' copy read "in". Georgia's earlier prints spell "expence" and Candler's ends the article "point out and direct."; Thorpe's "expense" and "point out." are kept. Vermont's State Archives text has a comma where Thorpe has a semicolon, after "paid by each town"; Thorpe's is kept. North Carolina's State Records capitalize its nouns. New Hampshire's text is Wikisource's transcription of Thorpe exactly. Massachusetts's chapter is the Commonwealth's text, as the charters' Declaration of Rights is: its words are Thorpe's in the same order, with 23 marks of its own and its bold for three words Thorpe sets in small capitals, and one word differs from the 1780 print, "legislatures" for its "legislators". A space the site's markup loses after "College," is put back, its cross-reference to later amendments is left out, and its square brackets round Article III of Section I, its mark for words annulled or superseded, are left out with a note saying so, since it names no amendment that changed them and neither Thorpe nor the 1780 print has them.

**The Land Ordinance.** The Library's transcription differed from the print in four places, each set right from both copies' images: the print's misprint "tne" kept as printed, "tots" for "lots", a missing space in "(A. B.) Commissioner", and a missing comma after "expedient". The broadside differs in 53 words and Folwell's print in 30, each its own reading; the Journals' stand at every one. The school lot is printed "the lot N 16, of every township, for the maintenance of public schools, within the said township". The blanks in the two deed forms are kept as wide spaces.

**The Ohio Company's terms.** The Journals print them not as a resolution in the day's proceedings but as a committee's report as amended, which the editor added from the Committee Book; the file takes the report's opening line and its ten terms, and leaves out the editor's heading and footnote. One word of the Library's transcription was set right, "fights" for "rights". Against the 1787 print, fifteen real differences ("Scioto" for "Sioto", "No. 16" for "N 16", "as aforesaid" for "of aforesaid"), the Journals' reading standing at each. A dot after "from" on p. 399, far smaller than a stop, is taken for a flaw of the plate, as the 1787 print has no stop there.

**The Ohio Enabling Act.** Wikisource's transcription had two slips, "ordnance" for "ordinance" and a missing comma after "elected", both settled in the two scans. The text came out byte for byte the same built from the Library's scan alone. The Annals and Thorpe edit the words ("entitled", "net", "Chillicothe"); the Statutes read intituled, nett, Chilicothe. The school grant reads "the section, number sixteen, in every township", with a comma after "section".

**The attendance act.** The State Library's transcription had one slip, "aet" for "act." at the end of section 5, settled against the letter forms of the page and both reprints. The two reprints share lighter punctuation in about seven places and shorten the approval line; the Acts and Resolves' readings are kept.

**Jefferson's letter.** The Library's transcription and the Papers differ in four readings, and the file follows the Papers in each: "messrs.", "& zealous", "adhering" in lower case, and "Th: Jefferson". The Library sets the date under the signature, as the file does.

**The Equal Access Act and the school prayer section.** The Code's words are the Acts', but for its own section numbers and catchlines and "this subchapter" or "this chapter" for the Acts' "this title" or "this Act".

**The Ten Commandments laws.** Texas's poster is Louisiana's word for word; Arkansas's is Louisiana's line for line but the first, which it sets in lower case; Alabama's gives another translation, without the first line and without stops. The codified Louisiana section differs from the Act in eighteen places, all the codifier's (among them "Summum" for the Act's "Summan" and "2009" for its "(2006)"); the file keeps the Act's words, slips included. Arkansas's Act prints struck and new words together; the file is the section as the Act made it. Alabama's keeps its own slips, "the establishment or religion" and "has born the phrase".

## Not settled

- **Georgia, 1777**: "point out." in Thorpe and Watkins's Digest, or "point out and direct." in Candler's print, which the next article also has; the convention's own printing of 1777 was not seen.
- **New Hampshire, 1784**: whether Thorpe's comma after "KNOWLEDGE", his "the magistrates" and his comma after "sentiments" come from another copy than the 1891 print he cites; the convention's printing of 1783 was not seen.
- **Jefferson's letter**: the four readings and the place of the date, to be confirmed on the Library of Congress's image of the press copy, which its site would not serve to a script or a browser on the day.
- **Alabama's Act**: no copy from another hand was found; the Act as signed, the codified section and the State Superintendent's approved poster would settle it.
- **Arkansas's and Texas's codified sections** were not read: Arkansas's official Code is published through a commercial service, and Texas's statutes site serves its pages to a browser only.
