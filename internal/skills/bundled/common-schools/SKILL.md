# Common Schools

This skill holds Irie's verified copies of texts on America's public schools, each in a file beside this one, kept as the charters are (the founding-documents skill): word for word from the body or print that keeps it, checked against another copy, every difference settled on the print's page images. `verification.md` says what each was checked against and what was found, and gives each file's SHA-512.

How public schools began:

- **massachusetts-school-law-1642.md**: the General Court's order of 14 June 1642 charging every town's chosen men to see that children are taught to read and understand the principles of religion and the capital laws, and put to some calling; in Shurtleff's Records (1853), in the colony's own spelling and contractions.
- **massachusetts-school-law-1647.md**: the order of 11 November 1647 that every township of fifty householders appoint a teacher and every town of a hundred families keep a grammar school; the same print.
- **pennsylvania-1776-section-44.md**, **north-carolina-1776-article-41.md**, **georgia-1777-article-54.md**, **vermont-1777-section-40.md**, **massachusetts-1780-chapter-5.md**, **new-hampshire-1784-literature.md**: the school provisions of the first state constitutions that called for schools, each the provision alone, in Thorpe's government print of 1909, and Massachusetts's chapter in the Commonwealth's own text.
- **land-ordinance-1785.md**: the Land Ordinance of 20 May 1785, which reserved "the lot N 16, of every township, for the maintenance of public schools", in the Journals of the Continental Congress.
- **ohio-company-terms-1787.md**: the terms of 23 July 1787 for the Ohio Company's purchase, which gave lot 16 in each township to those purposes and two townships for a university; the Journals.
- **ohio-enabling-act-1802.md**: the Act of 30 April 1802 that let Ohio form a state and offered it "the section, number sixteen, in every township" for the use of schools; the Statutes at Large.
- **massachusetts-attendance-act-1852.md**: Massachusetts's act of 18 May 1852 requiring children of eight to fourteen to attend a public school twelve weeks a year, the first general law of its kind; the Acts and Resolves of 1852.

Church and state in public schools:

- **jefferson-danbury-letter.md**: Jefferson's letter of 1 January 1802 to the Danbury Baptist Association, which read the First Amendment as "a wall of separation between Church & State".
- **equal-access-act.md**: the Equal Access Act of 1984, 20 U.S.C. §§ 4071–4074.
- **school-prayer-certification.md**: the ESEA's section on constitutionally protected prayer, 20 U.S.C. § 7904.
- **louisiana-ten-commandments-act.md**, **arkansas-ten-commandments-act.md**, **texas-ten-commandments-act.md**, **alabama-ten-commandments-act.md**: the four state laws of 2024 to 2026 that require the Ten Commandments in public school classrooms, as enacted.

The charters' Northwest Ordinance ("schools and the means of education shall forever be encouraged"), Mayflower Compact, Declaration, Virginia Statute, Constitution and Bill of Rights, which these texts draw on, are in the founding-documents skill.
