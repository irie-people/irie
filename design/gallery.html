Roles

A palette's 21 colours, each for one job. Text keeps 4.5:1 and the accent 3:1 on all eight surfaces; each tile gives its lowest. Parts name these roles, never colours (README §2).

States

Each state has a glyph and words of its own, so colour never carries it alone. Red is only for what needs you (principles §1.5, §1.7).

Agents' colours

Six hues 50° apart, clear of the needs red, at one low chroma. Cards side by side take hues at least 100° apart. Shown here at the library's starting lightness; each client then moves a mark only as far as 3.2:1 on its wash needs (tokens.json, agents).

Session cards

Flat at rest; washed while working; tinted red when it needs you; raised when selected. Under the pointer the age gives way to the card's buttons, and a boosted card keeps its boost button, filled, as its one mark (components §3.1, §3.2).

Controls

Counts, the task bar, buttons, the segmented control and a field. One prominent button per view; the rest are soft (components §2.3, §3.5 to §3.8).

Banners and notices

A banner stays while what it says is true; a notice comes and goes, with Undo where it can. The details panel holds what a card's two lines cannot (components §3.9, §3.10).

Principles

The rules these follow, each with its reason, source and check, are in principles.md; the parts in components.md. The ones that shape this page most:

  1. Lightness carries the meaning. §1.1
  2. Text reads at 4.5:1 and marks at 3:1, on every surface. §1.2
  3. Calm: low chroma, never neon. §1.4
  4. Red means one thing: it needs you. §1.5
  5. Colour is never the only cue. §1.7
  6. Only the person moves a card. §2.1
  7. Nothing moves under the pointer. §2.3
  8. Plain words first, the exact thing one step away. §3.1
  9. The button is the mark. §4.1
  10. Act at once, then offer undo. §4.2
